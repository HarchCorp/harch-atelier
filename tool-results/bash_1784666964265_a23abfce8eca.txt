## Phase 1 — Reasoning about what a HarchCorp trader actually needs

A HarchCorp trader sits at a multi-screen desk and their minute-by-minute concerns are very different from a risk analyst or comms officer. They care about (in priority order):

1. **The Casablanca board at a glance** — MASI index intraday ticks (is the market up or down today?), 30-day trend (are we in a rally or pullback?), session breadth (advancers vs decliners), and turnover (is there liquidity?). Without this they can't even start their day.
2. **Casablanca-listed equities** — real tickers (ATW, IAM, BOA, CIH, LBV, OLC, MNG, LHM, OCP, ADH, RIS, etc.) with last price, %chg, volume, market cap, P/E. They need a sortable screener they can filter by sector. Clicking a row should reveal a 30-day sparkline + key stats so they can decide whether to add to the book.
3. **Their own book** — HarchCorp's open positions with mark-to-market P&L, exposure by sector, long/short split, win rate. The blotter is the single source of truth for "are we making or losing money today?". Critical: M2M P&L must be directional (long = (last-avg)*qty, short = (avg-last)*qty).
4. **Sector context** — which sector is leading/lagging the MASI? A trader needs to know if banking is the engine today or if materials are pulling the index down. Sector heatmap + comparison chart normalised to 100.
5. **FX & rates** — MAD crosses (EUR/MAD, USD/MAD, GBP/MAD) because HarchCorp has FX exposure; Bank Al-Maghrib key rate because it sets the cost of capital; MAD strength index for export-competitiveness read.
6. **Commodities exposure** — phosphate (OCP is a top Moroccan listing), Brent (energy import), gold (safe-haven hedge), wheat (food import), nat gas, silver. With HarchCorp notional exposure column to bridge "what's the position impact".
7. **Fixed income** — BAM T-bill yield curve (13W→15Y), 2s10s spread (curve steepness), and a corporate bonds table for the credit book.

Plus — and this is the intelligence bridge — they need the existing risk widgets (Risk Matrix, Media Coverage, Sentiment, Share of Voice, Risk Events, Watchlist, Activity Feed) to be navigable because risk events impact positions. A trader who reads "Ransomware affiliate claims exfiltration" needs to see immediately whether their tech-bank position is exposed.

The user's "beaucoup beaucoup de graphique" mandate means every section needs at least 3–5 chart panels: line, area, bar, heatmap, scatter. No section is just a table.

## Stage Summary

- **Built (14 sections)**: 7 trader markets sections (`mkt-bvc`, `mkt-equities`, `mkt-positions`, `mkt-indices`, `mkt-fx`, `mkt-commodities`, `mkt-fixed-income`) + 7 intel sections (`intel-risk-matrix`, `intel-coverage`, `intel-sentiment`, `intel-sov`, `intel-events`, `intel-watchlist`, `intel-activity`).
- **Left as placeholder** (for downstream role-agents): `intel-overview`, `intel-alerts`, all `risk-*` (10), all `comms-*` (8), all `ent-*` (5), all `admin-*` (5). The dispatcher falls back to `SectionPlaceholder` automatically.
- **Mock dataset shape**: see `src/lib/market-data.ts` — the next role-agents (admin/legal/market/pr) can reuse `BvcSector`, `Equity`, `Position`, `FxPoint`, `Commodity`, `YieldCurvePoint`, `CorporateBond`, `sectorColor`, `sectorChipTint`, `formatMAD`, `chgColor`, `chgBgClass`. The dataset is self-contained and typed (no `any`), so reusing it elsewhere is a single import.
- **Architectural decisions**: (1) `section-dispatcher.tsx` is the single entry — page.tsx never imports section components directly. (2) `SectionHeader` is the universal chrome — every built section starts with it so visual language matches the placeholder. (3) Intel sections are thin wrappers (≤25 LOC each) that delegate to existing dashboard widgets — no logic duplication. (4) Trader accent = emerald (per project palette); FX/Commodities/Fixed-Income use cyan/amber/violet tints to distinguish subdomains while staying within the project palette.
- **Issues/risks**: (1) `masiSessionStats.advancers + decliners + unchanged = 81` is hardcoded as the divisor for breadth-bar widths — if the underlying counts change, the magic number 81 must update. (2) The yield curve chart's Y-domain is fixed `[2.2, 3.8]` to keep the curve readable; if real yields move outside that range, the chart will clip. (3) The corporate bonds table uses a hand-rolled `<table>` (not shadcn Table) for the indices performance table in `indices-view.tsx` — minor inconsistency with other tables but functionally equivalent. (4) The `IntelEvents` section's `RiskEventsTable` is the same component as the dashboard — switching between them resets the table's local filter state. Acceptable since the global `risk-store` filters persist.
