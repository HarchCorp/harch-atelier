    74→    74→  - `scheduleNextUpdate()`: every 4–7s (randomised per-tick via `randInt(4000,7000)`), picks a random signal id, mutates `delta` by ±0.1–±1.5 (rounded to 2 decimals, clamped to ±10), bumps `articles` by +1–+8, refreshes `updatedAt` to now, and with a 10% chance flips `severity` one level up or down (within `low → medium → high → critical`). Emits `signal:update` with the single patched signal. Uses `setTimeout` recursion so the cadence is genuinely random rather than a fixed interval.
    75→    75→  - `scheduleNextKpiTick()`: every 30s (via `setInterval`), drifts `riskIndex` ±0.4, `negativeShare` ±0.6pp, and `activeAlerts` by −1/0/+1 (weighted), then emits `kpis:tick` with the new values + a fresh `ts` ISO string.

   824→   824→## Phase 1 — Reasoning about what a HarchCorp trader actually needs
   825→   825→
   826→   826→A HarchCorp trader sits at a multi-screen desk and their minute-by-minute concerns are very different from a risk analyst or comms officer. They care about (in priority order):
   827→   827→
   828→   828→1. **The Casablanca board at a glance** — MASI index intraday ticks (is the market up or down today?), 30-day trend (are we in a rally or pullback?), session breadth (advancers vs decliners), and turnover (is there liquidity?). Without this they can't even start their day.
   829→   829→2. **Casablanca-listed equities** — real tickers (ATW, IAM, BOA, CIH, LBV, OLC, MNG, LHM, OCP, ADH, RIS, etc.) with last price, %chg, volume, market cap, P/E. They need a sortable screener they can filter by sector. Clicking a row should reveal a 30-day sparkline + key stats so they can decide whether to add to the book.
   830→   830→3. **Their own book** — HarchCorp's open positions with mark-to-market P&L, exposure by sector, long/short split, win rate. The blotter is the single source of truth for "are we making or losing money today?". Critical: M2M P&L must be directional (long = (last-avg)*qty, short = (avg-last)*qty).
   831→   831→4. **Sector context** — which sector is leading/lagging the MASI? A trader needs to know if banking is the engine today or if materials are pulling the index down. Sector heatmap + comparison chart normalised to 100.
   832→   832→5. **FX & rates** — MAD crosses (EUR/MAD, USD/MAD, GBP/MAD) because HarchCorp has FX exposure; Bank Al-Maghrib key rate because it sets the cost of capital; MAD strength index for export-competitiveness read.
   833→   833→6. **Commodities exposure** — phosphate (OCP is a top Moroccan listing), Brent (energy import), gold (safe-haven hedge), wheat (food import), nat gas, silver. With HarchCorp notional exposure column to bridge "what's the position impact".
   834→   834→7. **Fixed income** — BAM T-bill yield curve (13W→15Y), 2s10s spread (curve steepness), and a corporate bonds table for the credit book.
   835→   835→
   836→   836→Plus — and this is the intelligence bridge — they need the existing risk widgets (Risk Matrix, Media Coverage, Sentiment, Share of Voice, Risk Events, Watchlist, Activity Feed) to be navigable because risk events impact positions. A trader who reads "Ransomware affiliate claims exfiltration" needs to see immediately whether their tech-bank position is exposed.
   837→   837→
   838→   838→The user's "beaucoup beaucoup de graphique" mandate means every section needs at least 3–5 chart panels: line, area, bar, heatmap, scatter. No section is just a table.
